Complete dental care

Support for a healthier smile, at every age

From routine examinations to more involved treatment, understanding your options is an important part of good dental care. Explore the services below to learn what they involve and when they may be considered.

01 / Preventive care

Prevention begins with everyday care

Preventive dentistry focuses on keeping teeth and gums healthy and identifying concerns early. Regular dental examinations give a dentist an opportunity to check for tooth decay, gum inflammation, changes in the mouth and other issues that may not be obvious at home.

A professional cleaning removes plaque and hardened deposits (tartar) from areas that brushing and flossing may miss. Your dental professional can also discuss brushing technique, interdental cleaning, fluoride and habits that may affect your oral health.

Preventive visits may include:

  • Examination of teeth, gums and soft tissues
  • Professional scaling and polishing when appropriate
  • Personalised oral-hygiene advice
  • Dental X-rays only when clinically indicated
How often you need a check-up depends on your oral health and individual risk factors. Your dentist can recommend a suitable interval.
02 / Restorative dentistry

Restore comfort, strength and function

Tooth decay, cracks, wear and injury can affect how a tooth looks and works. Restorative dentistry aims to repair or replace damaged tooth structure, preserve useful natural teeth where possible and help restore comfortable chewing.

Depending on the size and location of the problem, a dentist may recommend a filling, an inlay or onlay, a crown, or a bridge. The choice depends on the clinical findings, the amount of remaining tooth structure, your bite and your preferences.

Common restorative options:

  • Fillings: repair areas affected by decay or minor damage.
  • Crowns: cover and protect a tooth that needs additional support.
  • Bridges: replace one or more missing teeth by attaching to supporting teeth or implants.
  • Inlays and onlays: restore selected areas when a standard filling may not be suitable.
A restoration does not make a tooth immune to future decay. Good home care and recommended follow-up remain important.
03 / Cosmetic dentistry

Enhance your smile while respecting your teeth

Cosmetic dentistry addresses the appearance of teeth and the smile. People may seek advice about tooth colour, small chips, uneven edges, gaps or the shape of visible teeth. A consultation helps establish what is causing the concern and which options are appropriate.

Professional whitening can lighten natural tooth colour, while bonding or veneers may be considered for selected shape or surface concerns. A dentist should explain the benefits, limitations, maintenance and potential effects on tooth structure before treatment begins.

Options that may be discussed:

  • Professional teeth whitening for suitable natural teeth
  • Composite bonding to repair selected chips or reshape edges
  • Veneers for specific colour or shape concerns
  • Smile planning that considers oral health, facial features and your goals
Whitening does not change the colour of crowns, fillings or veneers. Not every cosmetic procedure is suitable for every patient.
04 / Orthodontics

Plan a healthier, more comfortable bite

Orthodontic treatment moves teeth gradually to improve alignment and, where possible, the way the upper and lower teeth meet. Treatment may be considered for crowding, spacing, selected bite problems or teeth that are difficult to clean because of their position.

Options can include fixed braces or removable clear aligners. The appropriate approach depends on the type and complexity of tooth movement needed, oral health, age and the patient's ability to follow the treatment plan.

What to know before starting:

  • An assessment is needed to understand the bite and tooth positions.
  • Treatment time varies; no single duration applies to everyone.
  • Keeping teeth and gums clean is especially important during treatment.
  • Retainers are commonly recommended after active treatment to help maintain alignment.
A dentist or orthodontist can explain expected changes, alternatives, risks and retention needs for your particular case.
05 / Children's dentistry

Positive dental experiences for growing smiles

Children benefit from dental care that is calm, age-appropriate and focused on prevention. Early visits help families receive guidance on cleaning baby teeth, fluoride, nutrition, habits and the development of the mouth. They can also help a child become familiar with the dental setting.

Depending on a child's needs, care may include monitoring tooth development, preventive treatments, fillings or advice about dental injuries. The dental team can explain each step in language a child can understand and involve parents or caregivers in decisions.

Ways families can support oral health:

  • Help children brush twice daily with an age-appropriate amount of fluoride toothpaste.
  • Encourage water and balanced meals, and limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks.
  • Ask the dentist about protective treatments such as sealants when suitable.
  • Seek advice promptly after a tooth injury or persistent pain.
The timing and type of care depend on the child's age, development and individual dental needs.
06 / Dental implants

Explore options for replacing missing teeth

A missing tooth can affect chewing, speech or confidence, and may change how nearby teeth meet. Dental implants are one option for replacing missing teeth. An implant is a small post placed in the jawbone to support a crown, bridge or denture.

Implant treatment involves careful planning. A dentist or implant clinician assesses oral health, bone volume, medical history and the condition of nearby teeth. Some patients may need additional procedures, while others may be offered a different tooth-replacement option.

Topics to discuss at a consultation:

  • Whether an implant is suitable for your mouth and health history
  • Expected stages and healing time
  • Possible risks, maintenance and long-term review
  • Alternatives such as bridges or removable dentures
Implants require ongoing cleaning and professional maintenance. Longevity varies and cannot be guaranteed.
07 / Root canal treatment

When possible, help save a natural tooth

Inside each tooth is a space containing soft tissue called the pulp. Deep decay, a crack or trauma can damage or infect this tissue. Root canal treatment removes the affected pulp, cleans and shapes the inside of the tooth, and seals it to help prevent reinfection.

The need for treatment is determined through an examination and, when appropriate, dental imaging. Local anaesthesia is commonly used. After treatment, a tooth may need a filling or crown depending on how much healthy structure remains.

After treatment:

  • Follow the clinician's instructions about eating and caring for the tooth.
  • Temporary tenderness can occur; ask the clinic what to expect.
  • Attend follow-up visits and complete any recommended final restoration.
  • Contact the clinic if pain or swelling worsens or you have concerns.
Not every tooth can be saved with root canal treatment. Your dentist can discuss prognosis and alternatives, including extraction, if needed.
08 / Urgent dental concerns

Get guidance when a dental problem cannot wait

Severe toothache, a broken tooth, swelling or an injury may need prompt dental assessment. If you contact a clinic, describe what happened, when it started and whether you have swelling, fever or difficulty opening your mouth. The team can advise on the next available step.

For a knocked-out permanent tooth, seek urgent dental care immediately. Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root. If it is dirty, rinse it gently with milk or saline if available; do not scrub it. If possible, keep it moist in milk while travelling to urgent care. Do not attempt to reinsert a baby tooth.

Seek emergency medical help if you have:

  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • Rapidly spreading swelling of the face or neck
  • Uncontrolled bleeding or a serious facial injury
This demo page does not provide live emergency triage. Contact local emergency services for potentially life-threatening symptoms.

Helpful answers

Frequently asked questions

General information to help you prepare. Your dentist can answer questions specific to your oral health and treatment needs.

How often should I have a dental check-up?

There is no single interval that suits everyone. Your dentist can recommend how often you should attend based on your oral health, history and risk of dental disease.

What happens during a professional cleaning?

A dental professional checks your teeth and gums and may remove plaque and hardened deposits. Polishing may be included when appropriate. You can mention sensitivity or discomfort at any time.

Are dental X-rays required at every visit?

No. Dental X-rays should be recommended when they are likely to help with diagnosis or treatment planning. Your dentist considers your individual needs and uses appropriate precautions.

Can teeth whitening damage my teeth?

Whitening can cause temporary sensitivity or gum irritation, and it is not suitable for everyone. A dental professional can assess your teeth and explain appropriate options and precautions.

Do dental fillings last forever?

No restoration lasts forever. Its lifespan depends on factors such as its size and location, bite forces, oral hygiene and habits. Regular examinations help your dentist monitor existing restorations.

Is a root canal treatment painful?

Local anaesthesia is commonly used to help manage discomfort during treatment. Some tenderness may occur afterwards. Your dentist can explain what to expect and how to contact the clinic if symptoms concern you.

Are dental implants suitable for everyone?

Not always. Suitability depends on factors including oral health, bone support, general health and personal circumstances. An assessment is needed to discuss implants and alternatives.

At what age should a child first see a dentist?

Professional guidance commonly recommends a first dental visit by the child's first birthday or within six months after the first tooth appears. Ask a local dental professional for advice suited to your child.

What should I do if I have a sudden toothache?

Contact a dental clinic for advice and an assessment, especially if pain is severe or persistent. Facial swelling, fever or difficulty opening the mouth should be reported promptly. Difficulty breathing or swallowing requires emergency medical help.

How can I keep my teeth healthy at home?

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth daily, limit frequent sugary foods and drinks, and follow the check-up schedule recommended for you.

Can I ask about treatment costs before starting?

Yes. You can ask for an explanation of proposed treatment, estimated costs, alternatives and what may affect the final amount before deciding whether to proceed.
